# Irish pub ladder

## Style language

The ladder is a board pinned by the bar of a Boston Irish pub. Bottle green
walls, cream card, brass fittings. Type is set like a painted pub sign: a fat
Clarendon slab for names and headlines, a bold condensed sans in caps for
numbers and labels, a book serif for running text. Ornament is limited to gold
double rules, brass roundels, and one hand-drawn paddle. No shamrocks, no
harps, no Celtic knots.

The leaderboard is literally a ladder: two dark rails, one rung per player,
a cream name plate hung on each rung. Rank 1 is at the top of the wall.
Wins are gold, losses are oxblood, paper is warmer than white.

## References

- London Museum, Guinness's iconic early adverts:
  https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/guinness-iconic-early-adverts/
  Took: bold uppercase slogans in one colour on a plain ground, a single
  drawn figure doing one thing, a slogan short enough to shout.
- Bendigo Squash Club challenge ladder:
  http://www.bendigosquash.com/challenge-ladder
  Took: the physical board with name tabs between the courts and recent
  matches posted beside it. The ladder motif comes from here.
- Creative Market, old-school fonts for vintage sign art:
  https://creativemarket.com/blog/20-old-school-fonts-for-creating-vintage-sign-art
  Took: fat slab and shadowed capitals as the pub-sign register; rejected
  the curlicue Victorian faces as too costume.
- The Black Rose, Faneuil Hall, Boston:
  https://www.blackroseboston.com/
  Took: dark ground, gold accent, restrained type. Irishness carried by
  warmth and hospitality rather than clichés.

## Palette

- Bottle green `#0E3A2C` (ground), deep green `#082A1F` (rails, shadows)
- Cream `#F4EAD3` (paper), aged cream `#E9DCBB` (plates)
- Brass `#C9A24B`, brass highlight `#EBCB73`
- Oxblood `#8A2E2A` (losses, paddle rubber)
- Ink `#1C1A16`

## Typefaces

- Headlines and names: Alfa Slab One (fallback Rockwell, Georgia bold)
- Numbers and labels: Oswald 500/700, uppercase, tracked (fallback
  Arial Narrow, Impact)
- Body: Libre Baskerville (fallback Georgia)

## Why this suits a dorm ladder

A residence ladder lives or dies on people wanting to be seen on it. A pub
board is public, a little theatrical, and unembarrassed about ranking
friends against each other. The warm palette says this is a social fixture
rather than a tracking tool, the brass rank roundels make third place feel
like something, and the printed-programme voice fits a house that already
posts notices by the table. The poster and the site look like the same object.
