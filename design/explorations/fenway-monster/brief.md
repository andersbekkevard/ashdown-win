# Fenway Monster

The site is the hand-operated scoreboard set into the Green Monster. Everything sits on a dark green painted wall. Numbers are cream metal plates dropped into rectangular slots, so each plate carries an inset shadow on its top and left edge. Names are painted block capitals in white, the way "BOS" and "NYY" are painted next to the slots. Small round lamps in red and green do the job of the ball, strike and out lights: a green lamp for a win, a red one for a loss, a dark unlit lamp for nothing yet. A yellow plate marks an "inning in progress", which the ladder uses for a player who has not played a match yet. Thin white painted rules divide sections, and the vertical divider stripe carries the word ASHDOWN in Morse code, after the Yawkey initials hidden in the real board. Corners get bolt heads. Nothing glows; the board is lit by daylight.

## References

- SABR, "Fenway Park's Hand-Operated Scoreboard" (https://sabr.org/journal/article/fenway-parks-hand-operated-scoreboard/). Took the plate system: 16×16 in and 12×16 in metal panels, numbers on both faces, a yellow digit for the current inning replaced by white when it closes, operators watching through a ten-inch viewing slot.
- Fenway Fanatics, Manual Scoreboard (https://www.fenwayfanatics.com/fenway-park/features/manual-scoreboard/). Took the green and red lamps for balls, strikes and outs, and the row structure with painted labels beside the slots.
- Wikipedia, Green Monster (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Monster). Took the 1947 green paint over earlier advertising, the 127 slots, and the Morse code in the white stripes.
- Boston.com and Benjamin Moore, Green Monster Green SC-12 (https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2014/08/20/now-you-can-paint-your-apartment-green-monster-green/). Confirmed the wall's "Fence Green" is proprietary and dark with a blue cast; SC-12 is the public approximation the palette below is tuned toward.
- WBEZ, "The Design of the Wrigley Scoreboard" (https://www.wbez.org/curious-city/2015/09/11/the-design-of-the-wrigley-scoreboard-revolutionary-retro-or-both). Took the forest green and off-white pairing as the genre baseline, and the idea of numerals sized for reading at distance.
- FunTrivia and Fonts In Use on the Fenway lettering (https://fontsinuse.com/typefaces/7583/fenway-park-jf). Confirmed the board's letters are hand-painted square block gothic, custom rather than a commercial face.

## Typefaces

- Plates and numerals: Alfa Slab One (Google Fonts), fallback Rockwell, then serif bold.
- Painted capitals and labels: Oswald 500/700, fallback Impact or Arial Narrow, always uppercase with wide tracking.
- Explanatory prose: system serif (Georgia, Times), as on a printed scorecard.

## Why it suits a dorm ladder

A manual board is the opposite of a live feed: it is only as current as the last person who walked behind the wall and moved a plate, which is exactly how a no-account ladder works. Someone records a match, and the numbers change because a person said so. The plates make ratings feel like objects that can be swapped, not decimals on a server, and the red and green lamps give win and loss a physical, unambiguous reading from across the room. Fenway is Boston's civic furniture; a house at MIT borrowing it says the table is a local institution too.
