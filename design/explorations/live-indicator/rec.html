Round 2 — the recording button

Four takes on one grammar: a dot plus a short word, the thing every camera, every screen recorder and every live feed uses to say "this is happening right now". Each is drawn in the Fall Guys language — Titan One, ink #222126 outlines, hard unblurred shadows — and each is shown in the four requested colours so the hue can be judged separately from the shape.

States throughout: live, a match inside the last 30 minutes; recent, within the last few hours; quiet, nothing today. All four go to the match log page and none is labelled "Log".

Each option also carries a min-width so its three states are all the same width and the chips never shift when the state changes.

A — Viewfinder badge, mint

The one shape here that is not a pill. A camcorder puts REC in a hard-cornered box in the corner of the frame and blinks the dot at about one beat a second, and that squareness is most of why it reads as equipment rather than as a button. The blink is a true square wave — steps(1), no easing — so it snaps on and off instead of breathing, which is the difference between a camera and a notification. The word never moves; only the dot does. Mint rather than red because this option carries the largest area of flat colour of the four, and a 46 px slab of red sitting 8 px from a pink logo on a blue field is three hot hues arguing; mint is the one Fall Guys colour not already spoken for in this bar and it keeps the dark ink text.

B — Status-bar pill, gold

When an iPhone without a Dynamic Island is recording the screen, the clock turns into a solid red capsule — the status bar itself becomes the indicator. That is the borrowed move here: a filled pill with a white rim, the same rim the logo already wears, so it reads as system furniture rather than as content. The motion is a slow hard-edged rim that grows and fades every 1.7 s, no blur, matching the way Fall Guys buttons throw a rim on press. Gold rather than red for the same reason as A — this is a filled pill and red fills fight the logo — and gold has the advantage that it already means "the good stuff" in this app without meaning "first place", which is the crown's job. ON and OFF are the shortest true pair in the language and they keep the pill narrow.

C — Dot in a ring, REC red

The reference image, straight. A filled dot inside a ring with the word beside it, and no container around any of it — so nothing new is added to a bar that already holds a pink pill, a black chip and a blue field. Because the red is only a 7 px dot sitting inside a white ring, it is the one option where true REC red is safe: the eye reads it as a lamp, not as a second brand colour, and it cannot fight the logo at that size. The dot breathes down to 60% and back every 1.4 s, which is the record button at rest rather than a strobe. The state ladder is borrowed from a broadcast tally light, where red means on air and a different lamp means merely armed: filled dot for live, hollow dot for recent, empty ring for quiet — so the three states are told apart by the shape inside the ring and survive greyscale, colour blindness and a phone in bright sun.

D — Island capsule, REC red

On a modern iPhone the recording indicator is a black capsule with a red dot that swells open into a label and shrinks back. This is that, at chip scale: an ink capsule identical in height and colour to the existing "How?" chip, inside which a red dot stretches into a rounded slab and the word NOW fades up on top of it, then collapses back to a dot. The capsule's own width never changes, so nothing in the top bar shifts while it animates — the morph happens entirely inside a fixed 46 px box. Like C it spends its red on a small area, but unlike C it is a solid tappable object with an obvious hit area. The 4.6 s cycle is deliberately slow; a top-bar element that morphs every second would be unbearable by the third visit.
