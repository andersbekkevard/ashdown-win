Is the table busy?

Four takes on a tiny live-activity indicator for the ashdown.win top bar. Each one borrows its visual grammar wholesale from an application people already read without being taught, then re-cuts it in the Fall Guys language from globals.css: Titan One and Fredoka, #222126 outlines, hard unblurred shadows, pill shapes.

Three states throughout. Live — a match went in inside the last 30 minutes. Recent — a match within the last few hours. Quiet — nothing today. All four tap through to the match log page, and none of them uses the word "Log".

The space problem, measured. At 390 px the top bar has 354 px of usable width and the current contents already spend all of it: logo 233 px + chips 115 px + an 8 px gap = 356 px. Nothing can simply be added. So in every mock below the indicator takes the "Log" chip's slot — it goes to the same page — which frees about 50 px. Measured budget for the indicator: ≈48 px.

The broadcast badge

Grammar from YouTube & Twitch

A saturated pill carrying one short uppercase word is the most literal live signal on the internet, and it survives at any size because the word does all the work. YouTube's red LIVE pill and Twitch's channel badge both drop the same shape onto a thumbnail; when the stream ends the badge does not disappear, it becomes a relative timestamp. That is exactly the ladder's problem — three degrees of recency in one slot. So: LIVE while the paddles are still out, 2H once the badge is only reporting history, and a drained grey OFF when nothing has happened today. The pill is the shape the app already uses everywhere, so it needs no introduction; the live state beats once every 1.5 s and throws a hard white rim, which is a Fall Guys button press rather than a broadcast glow.

The presence dot

Grammar from Slack, Discord & GitHub

Chat apps solved the smallest possible status display years ago and did it without a single word. The state lives in the shape of the dot, not its colour: Slack fills it in when you are active and hollows it out when you are away, Discord bites a crescent out of it for idle. That is the cheapest signal on this page and the only one that costs nothing to internationalise, nothing to translate, and nothing in width. Here the dot sits on an ink coin the same 32 px as the existing chips, so it reads as one of the family rather than as a new species. Filled mint means someone is at the table now, and the whole coin breathes about six times a minute — slow enough to notice only if you look. Gold crescent means earlier today. Hollow grey ring means nobody has played. Wordless, so the "Log" restriction never comes up, with the sentence carried in the accessible label instead.

The busyness meter

Grammar from Google Maps popular times

Google Maps answers "is it worth walking over?" with a histogram, and the live version simply lights the bar for right now. It is a quantity, not an announcement, and that suits a ping-pong table better than a broadcast badge does: what you actually want to know at the top of the stairs is whether today has been busy and whether the last bar is still climbing. Four bars stand for the last four hours; the rightmost is the hour you are in. It goes tall and banana-yellow and rises and falls when a match has just landed, drops to a short mint stub for a couple of hours afterwards, and flattens to a grey floor when the day has been empty. The numeral beside it is the day's match count, which is the same numeral the leaderboard already speaks in. The ink pill matches the existing chips exactly, so the meter reads as data rather than as an alert.

The location beacon

Grammar from Find My & Uber

Find My and Uber both say "this is a real thing happening in a real place" with the same object: a solid dot throwing rings outward, with a lowercase relative timestamp beside it. The rings are the only animation on this page that leaves its own bounds, which is why the live state carries further than the others at a glance, and the wording stays in the register those apps use — now, 2h, an em dash for no signal. It has no pill around it, so it does not compete with the chips for the eye; it floats on the blue the way a location dot floats on a map. When the last match is a couple of hours old the rings freeze in place at half opacity, which is Find My's "last seen here" and reads as a memory of activity rather than activity. When nothing has happened the core goes pale and the rings vanish entirely.
