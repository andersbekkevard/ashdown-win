I understood ashdown.win as a public, no-account leaderboard for an Ashdown table-tennis ladder: create a name, find players, record who won, and watch Elo ratings change. The leaderboard, player count, wins/losses, rating numbers, and `CREATE PLAYER`/`RECORD MATCH` actions make that plausible quickly. In my first ten seconds, though, I could not tell that it was specifically table tennis, what Elo means, whether the numbers are current ratings rather than points, or that anyone can change the public log.

## Friction, highest cost first

1. **Steps 3 and 4 — submitted matches apparently vanished.** I expected `RECORD IT` to add my Alex Friction singles win and then my doubles result to the board/log. Each form returned to the board after roughly 3 seconds, but Alex and the new partner still showed “New. No matches yet”; neither result appeared in the log. I also double-tapped the doubles `RECORD IT` as the resilience test, with the same outcome. This cost two complete match-entry flows (about 35 taps/typing selections and several seconds) and makes the central action feel unsafe or broken. **Fix:** show a durable success confirmation identifying the match and rating deltas, then ensure the just-recorded match is visibly present; surface an actionable error if persistence fails. Screenshot: `/tmp/codex-friction/shot-4-singles.png`, `/tmp/codex-friction/shot-5-doubles.png`.

2. **Steps 3–4 — player selection is slow and tap-heavy.** Tapping a player field, typing, waiting through “Searching…”, then tapping the result is required for every slot. A doubles match requires four repetitions; creating a missing partner adds a modal and returns to the unfinished form. The form itself is understandable, but this cost around 10–15 seconds of waiting and 13 taps before Record (excluding typing) in doubles. **Fix:** preload a compact player picker or show immediate local filtering, and support a clear “new player” option in the field without a separate modal. Screenshot: `/tmp/codex-friction/shot-5-doubles.png`.

3. **Step 1 — the home page lacks an immediate plain-language purpose.** I expected a poster landing page to tell me what game and community it serves. “LEADERBOARD,” bare ratings, and names were clear, but “table tennis,” “Elo,” no-account/public editing, and how to start were not. This caused a 5–10 second hesitation. **Fix:** add one short lead, e.g. “Ashdown House table-tennis Elo ladder — add yourself, record a result.” Screenshot: `/tmp/codex-friction/shot-1-home.png`.

4. **Step 2 — name handling is mostly safe but not fully explained.** The privacy disclosure is helpful; duplicate `jchen26` produced a good direct message and link. Spaces disabled Create. An overly long name was silently truncated in the field, with no stated character limit, and the old duplicate error remained after changing the input. Emoji names worked. The stale error and unexplained truncation caused about 10 seconds of uncertainty. **Fix:** clear validation on edit and state/show the maximum length and remaining characters. Screenshot: `/tmp/codex-friction/shot-3-duplicate.png`.

5. **Step 5 — a new player page does not teach the graph until there is data.** I could identify rating, record, rank, and “matches newest first.” With no recorded result, `LAST 10`/`LAST 30` were disabled and the image said “drag across the line,” but there was no useful opportunity to learn the touch interaction. Because my recorded match did not persist, I could not complete meaningful range-tab or graph-touch testing. **Fix:** add a one-sentence empty-state explanation and make the graph interaction discoverable once data exists.

6. **Step 6 — deletion feels reasonably safe, but the consequence is easy to miss.** The log’s append-only explanation is excellent; Delete becomes “Delete this match?” with `Yes, delete` and `Keep`, then a `Restore` control appears. I understood it and could restore it, but the distinction between the original, deletion event, and restore event takes careful reading in a dense feed. This took 4 taps for delete-and-restore and about 10 seconds. **Fix:** add a short post-action status such as “Match #29 is void; ratings recomputed. Restore is available.” Screenshots: `/tmp/codex-friction/shot-6-log.png`, `/tmp/codex-friction/shot-7-delete.png`.

7. **Step 7 — the algorithm is trustworthy but overshoots a newcomer’s reading level.** After a minute, the concrete starting rating, K values, doubles half-weight, replayed log, and delete/restore explanation made me trust the approach. The opening says it takes five minutes and immediately presents dense notation; a resident who only wants confidence may bounce. **Fix:** begin with a three-sentence plain-English summary and keep the derivation below it. Screenshot: `/tmp/codex-friction/shot-8-algorithm.png`.

8. **Step 8 — resilience feedback is missing.** Back navigation showed a brief loading state before the board; reload at desktop width did not visibly fail. The double-tap test had no duplicate-protection feedback, and because the match did not show up, it was impossible to tell whether the tap was rejected, deduplicated, or lost. **Fix:** disable Record immediately with an explicit “Saving result…” state and then show success/error.

## Things that worked well

The visual hierarchy is compact and readable at 390×844. Creating a player is simple, the no-full-name guidance is concrete, duplicate detection offers the existing player, emoji is accepted, and the doubles switch clearly changes the model to 2v2. The append-only log explanation and confirmation/Restore controls give deletion more safety than a destructive-looking admin screen.
